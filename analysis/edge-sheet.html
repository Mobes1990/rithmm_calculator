The same questions, asked of both books

The NBA backtest and the live CFB season are independent samples with different models, so a pattern that appears in both is worth far more than one that appears in either alone. Most do not survive the crossing.

QuestionNBA 2024-25CFB 2026Verdict

Only one behavior holds in both: the models over-back the away side and the underdog, and that side loses. Everything sport-specific — which spread side pays, whether juice discipline shows up, where the totals edge sits — flips or vanishes. Treat each sport's book as its own problem.

Method & caveats

How these numbers were built

The two books

NBA: three model workbooks, 1,835 rows, 19 Nov 2024 – 29 Mar 2025, 535 games. CFB: the tracked-bets export, 2,752 rows, 29 Aug – 20 Sep 2026, 308 games across four models. Each is cleaned and analyzed separately; nothing is pooled across sports.

Cleaning

NBA: 2 ungraded rows and 23 exact duplicates removed, leaving 1,808. CFB: 435 exact duplicates removed, leaving 2,317, of which 107 are flagged degenerate — a model output at or beyond 99%/1%, or a price past ±2000, which carries no information about edge — leaving 2,210 analyzed. Results were re-derived from final scores and matched the recorded grade on every row.

DTM is not the same field in both

The NBA workbooks compute DTM = win% ÷ implied% − 1. The CFB export computes DTM = (win% − implied%) ÷ (100 − implied%) — edge as a share of the headroom above the market price. Both confirm on essentially every row, but DTM values are not comparable across the two books. 45 CFB rows disagree with their own posted price, i.e. the line moved after the number was produced.

Recommended vs. Caution

The CFB export carries Rithmm's own Recommended Bet field, so that book uses it directly. It is not a plain DTM threshold: every Recommended bet has DTM between 1.0 and 15.6, but 403 bets inside that band are not flagged, so a second condition exists that the export does not expose. The NBA workbooks have no such column, so that book falls back to DTM > 0 as the proxy. Smart Signal is False on all 2,752 CFB rows and carries no information.

ROI and units

Flat 1 unit per bet in both books. A win returns decimal odds − 1, a loss −1, a push 0, and pushes stay in the bet count. Win% is computed on decided bets; break-even% is the average implied probability of the prices actually taken. The CFB export stakes a flat $5 on every row, so its stake column carries no signal.

The honest limit

CFB is four weeks old — 308 games, and one bad Saturday moves any bucket. NBA is a partial season whose three models overlap on many of the same games, so cross-model agreement there is weaker evidence than it looks. Confidence intervals come from a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole games rather than individual bets. These are places to test forward with small stakes, not edges already proven.
