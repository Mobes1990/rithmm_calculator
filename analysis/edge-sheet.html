Method & caveats

How these numbers were built

Source and cleaning

The three workbooks' in sheets, concatenated: 1,835 rows. Two rows with no graded result and 23 exact duplicates (identical model, game, market, side, line, price and probability) were dropped, leaving 1,808. Spread, total and moneyline results were each re-derived from the final scores and matched the recorded bet result on 100% of rows.

What DTM is

Reverse-engineered from the data: DTM = (model win% ÷ market implied win% − 1) × 100, the model's edge as a percentage of the market's own price, with the vig left in. It confirms exactly on every row. Recommended here means DTM > 0 — the model likes the price; Caution means DTM ≤ 0.

Favorite vs. underdog

On spreads, from the sign of spread value, which is the number applied to the side actually bet: negative lays points, positive takes them. On moneylines, from the sign of the American price. Totals have no favorite, so they are split Over/Under instead.

ROI and units

Flat 1 unit per bet. A win returns decimal odds − 1, a loss −1, a push 0, and pushes stay in the bet count. Win% is computed on decided bets only; break-even% is the average implied probability of the prices actually taken.

Significance

Confidence intervals come from a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole games, not individual bets, because one game can carry three correlated wagers. The headline spread-underdog bucket lands at 95% CI −2.4% to +25.1%, P(ROI>0) = 0.95 — strong, but not airtight at n = 280.

The honest limit

One partial NBA season, 535 games, with the three models overlapping on many of them — so cross-model agreement is weaker evidence than it looks. Combined with ~2,800 slices scanned, the right read is: these are the places to test forward with small stakes, not edges already proven.
